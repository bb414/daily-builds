Still paying?

Find every subscription, direct debit and standing order running out of your bank account, see exactly when each one leaves next, and shut off the ones you no longer want before they renew.

Runs on your deviceNothing is uploadedNot financial advice

Step 1 of 4

Bring in your statements

Download a CSV export from your banking app or website (or an OFX or QIF file, if that is what your bank offers; most let you choose a date range: a full year finds yearly renewals too) and drop it here. Add as many accounts or months as you like; overlapping months are only counted once.

Drop bank exports here

CSV, TSV, OFX or QIF files from any bank app, card or spending app. You can also drop a saved review (.json) to carry on where you left off.

Paste a table instead

Copy the transactions table from your online banking (including its header row) and paste it here. Tabs, commas or semicolons all work.

Step 2 of 4

Every regular payment

Each tag is one payment that comes round on a schedule. Open a valve to see why it was picked out and every payment behind it, then decide: keep it, cancel it, look at it again, or mark it as not a cost (such as money to your own savings).

Step 3 of 4

When it all leaves

Every expected payment, worked out from each one's pattern. Direct debits and standing orders due on a weekend or bank holiday move to the next working day; card payments are shown on the day they are usually taken.

Step 4 of 4

Your plan, ready to take away

What cancelling saves, exactly what to do and by when, a message ready to send, and files for your calendar and spreadsheet. Save the review and reopen it next month to check that cancelled payments really stopped.

Under the floorboards

How it works, and its limits

Still paying? works entirely inside this page: your statements are read by your own browser and never sent anywhere. The sample statements belong to an invented student and every company in them is made up. This is a checking tool, not financial advice.
