How the model works
  • Cases and skill. Each simulated person answers yes-or-no cases of varying difficulty. Their chance of being right depends on their skill against the case's difficulty. The AI works the same way, and "shared blind spots" sets how far it finds the same cases hard.
  • Workflows. With AI first, people see the answer straight away and, the more they trust it, the more often they accept it without thinking. With human first, they always answer first, then may switch. With on request, they only ask when they feel unsure.
  • Switching. When they disagree with the AI, people weigh their own confidence against their trust in the AI and (if shown) how sure the AI sounds.
  • Trust. When people find out the right answer, trust rises after the AI is right and falls faster after it is wrong.
  • Own judgement. Thinking for yourself and getting feedback builds skill, more so after a visible disagreement. Accepting the AI without thinking lets skill fade.
  • The verdict. Every run is compared with the same people working without the AI. More than 1 point more accurate counts as a lift; ending more than 1 point worse on their own counts as eroded judgement.

All numbers are illustrative. The model shows how the mechanisms interact; it is not calibrated to any real organisation or study.

Skill Fade is a thinking tool built on stated assumptions, not a forecast. All data is synthetic and generated in your browser.
