How the model works
  • The organisation. People are split into teams and teams into departments. Each person has close contacts in their own team, a few in other teams of their department and fewer elsewhere, drawn at random to match the averages you set.
  • Champions use the tool from day one, never give up, and count as several users in the eyes of their contacts.
  • Trying it. Each week, a person whose job suits the tool becomes open to it once the share of their contacts using it passes their personal bar (between half and one and a half times the average social proof). Once open, each contact using it gives them a chance to try it. Anyone can also try it unprompted.
  • Dropping it. Users with no contacts using it drop it at the weekly rate you set; the more of their contacts use it, the less likely they are to stop.
  • Fair comparisons. Every strategy faces the same organisation and the same weekly luck in each rollout, so differences come from where the champions sit.
  • Strategies. Best connected picks the most contacts. Bridge builders picks the highest betweenness (how many shortest paths between others run through a person). One team first fills the most tightly knit team, then the next. One per team takes the best-connected person in each team, most tightly knit teams first.

All numbers are illustrative. The model shows how network structure and social proof interact; it is not calibrated to any real organisation.

Champion Seeding is a thinking tool built on stated assumptions, not a forecast. Every organisation in it is synthetic and generated in your browser.
