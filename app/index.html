When it happens

Qualitative to quantitative

DSIT describes a spectrum of assurance, from judgement-based assessments to objective measurement, used in combination.

All techniques

Coverage is the chance the technique catches a material problem of that kind, if there is one. Set any technique to Must, Done (you already have it) or Rule out.

How it works

Risk. Each of the five principles gets a likelihood, the chance a material problem exists (1 to 5, from under 5% to over 80%), and an impact, how bad it would be (1 to 5). Both start at 1 and your answers add to them. The score is likelihood times impact, the same product the DSIT AI risk management toolkit uses.

Assurance. A technique does not change what is at stake. It changes the chance that a problem reaches people unnoticed. Each technique has a coverage for each principle; problems a chosen technique catches are assumed to be fixed. The chance a problem slips through every chosen technique is the product of their misses, and that chance is turned back into a likelihood score.

Search. Every combination of the usable techniques is scored (up to 65,536). The cheapest plan is the lowest-cost combination that brings every principle within your appetite and goes live in time; equal costs go to the lower worst score, then fewer techniques. The lowest-risk plan within budget minimises the worst principle; ties go to the lower total across all five, then the lower cost.

Pilot to scale. The same system is planned at each roll-out stage, keeping what earlier stages bought (it costs nothing again and takes no time). This is compared with finding the cheapest full-scale plan first and phasing it in.

Robustness. Each of 400 draws scales every technique's coverage by a random factor, up to 25% either way for in-house work and 40% for independent assurers. The same draws are used for every plan. The robust plan is the cheapest plan that meets your appetite on the assumed coverages and stays within it in at least the chosen share of draws.

Time. Design work comes first, then testing before go-live; techniques in the same stage run side by side, and one that needs another waits for it. Monitoring starts at go-live.

Limitations

All costs, durations and coverages are illustrative judgements, not market data. Techniques are assumed to catch problems independently, which flatters combinations of similar techniques. One-off costs and a first year of monitoring are counted; the cost of fixing what is found is not. The questions add fixed amounts to likelihood and impact; a real assessment would judge each system on its own facts.
