When it happens

Qualitative to quantitative

DSIT describes a spectrum of assurance, from judgement-based assessments to objective measurement, used in combination.

All techniques

Coverage is the chance the technique catches a material problem of that kind, if there is one. Set any technique to Must or Rule out.

How it works

Risk. Each of the five principles gets a likelihood, the chance a material problem exists (1 to 5, from under 5% to over 80%), and an impact, how bad it would be (1 to 5). Both start at 1 and your answers add to them. The score is likelihood times impact, the same product the DSIT AI risk management toolkit uses.

Assurance. A technique does not change what is at stake. It changes the chance that a problem reaches people unnoticed. Each technique has a coverage for each principle; problems a chosen technique catches are assumed to be fixed. The chance a problem slips through every chosen technique is the product of their misses, and that chance is turned back into a likelihood score.

Search. Every combination of the usable techniques is scored (up to 65,536). The cheapest plan is the lowest-cost combination that brings every principle within your appetite and goes live in time; the lowest-risk plan within budget minimises the worst principle. Ties go to the lower total risk, then the lower cost, then fewer techniques.

Time. Design work comes first, then testing before go-live; techniques in the same stage run side by side, and one that needs another waits for it. Monitoring starts at go-live.

Limitations

All costs, durations and coverages are illustrative judgements, not market data. Techniques are assumed to catch problems independently, which flatters combinations of similar techniques. One-off costs and a first year of monitoring are counted; the cost of fixing what is found is not.
