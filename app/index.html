How Save or sink works

Each simulated year runs from Monday 1 September to 31 August. Student loan payments arrive at the start of each term, wages arrive on Fridays for the hours worked that week, rent leaves on the 1st and bills on the 10th of each month, and everyday spending, nights out and the odd unexpected cost are drawn at random around the amounts you set. Every saving rule runs through exactly the same years, so differences come from the rules alone.

Each rule moves money from the current account into a savings pot at the end of the day. If the current account then falls below your comfort point, you move money back from the pot (a "raid"). Overdraft interest is charged only beyond the interest-free limit, and savings interest is paid on the pot. Both are simple annual rates, charged or paid monthly.

The verdict and the tuning map call a rule safe when it is no more than 2 percentage points more likely than not saving to go past the limit in a year, spends no more than one extra day a year past it on average, and does not leave the student worse off in a typical year. "Within £100 of my limit" means as soon as you are overdrawn if the limit itself is under £100.

The smart saver looks at the last 28 days of card spending, projects it forward over its forecast window, adds rent and bills it knows are coming, keeps a safety buffer, and saves a share of whatever is left. Its blind spot is anything outside that window: a two-week forecast made the day after a loan payment sees a full account and none of the rent due over the rest of the term.

The term planner does the same sums but always looks as far as the next loan payment (or 31 August after the last one), so every rent payment that loan has to cover is in view. It saves less at the start of term and gets raided far less.

The tuning map runs one rule many times with different settings over the same years, so you can see which dial changes the risk and which only changes the amount. The Python package does the same over more years and marks the best trade-offs: settings where nothing else leaves the student better off without also going past the limit in more years or being raided more often.

Save file downloads the settings as JSON, Open file reads them back, and Copy link puts them in the page address. The Python package reads both, so the same settings can be run over thousands of years from the command line.

Everything is calculated in this page and nothing is sent anywhere. The students, banks and amounts are invented, and this is a teaching tool, not financial advice. The same settings always give the same answer.
