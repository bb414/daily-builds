How good is each warning light?

Across 36,672 player-weeks, 1.0% ended in a soft-tissue injury within seven days. The best real light, the risk model (the AI), scores 0.62 (0.5 is a coin toss, 1 is perfect); perfect knowledge of the hidden truth reaches 0.67. At its rule, the risk model (the AI) flags 12.2% of player-weeks and catches 24% of those injuries, but only 1.8 of every 100 flags are followed by one. A single season can score it anywhere from 0.50 to 0.71 on luck alone, and 62% of injuries come from contact, which no load figure foresees.

Could a one-season pilot tell?

With the luck held fixed, rest anyone flagged changes injuries by -2.4 a season (-1.3 soft-tissue). But one season of injuries varies by about 5.5 either way, so a before-and-after pilot swings by 7.3: it shows fewer injuries 57% of the time and more 43% of the time. Detecting the true effect four times in five needs 84 seasons in each arm. League points change by -4.5 a season. 97% of acted-on flags are followed by a quiet fortnight, which looks like success to a coach; in truth it took about 58 acted-on flags to prevent one injury.

Five ways to manage the squad

Over 30 seasons with the same luck, against picking the best and ignoring the light: rest anyone flagged changed injuries by -2.8 (-3.5 to -2.1), days lost by -53 and league points by -4.1; cut training when flagged changed injuries by -0.9 (-1.4 to -0.4), days lost by -11 and league points by -1.2; planned rotation changed injuries by -0.9 (-1.7 to -0.1), days lost by -10 and league points by -5.3; pick on readiness changed injuries by -0.3 (-1.0 to +0.2), days lost by -10 and league points by +0.3. Rest anyone flagged lost the fewest days and pick on readiness won the most points.
